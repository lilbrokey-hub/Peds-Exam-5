## PART 1: GENETIC DISORDERS

### GENERAL INFORMATION: SIGNIFICANT FINDINGS IN MEDICAL HISTORY

Important indicators that a child may have a genetic disorder:
- Maternal age older than 35 years or paternal age older than 50
- Repeated premature births, breech delivery
- Congenital hip dysplasia
- Abnormalities on ultrasound or prenatal blood tests
- Amniotic fluid abnormalities
- Multiple births
- Exposure to medications and known teratogens
- Decreased fetal movement

---

### DOWN SYNDROME (TRISOMY 21)

#### Definition
- Chromosomal abnormality consisting of:
  - **Extra chromosome**: Three instead of two number 21 chromosomes
  - **Translocation**: Genetic material incorrectly located
  - **Mosaicism**: Some cells are normal and some have trisomy 21

#### Associated Medical Conditions
- Congenital heart defects
- Respiratory tract infections
- Congenital hypothyroidism
- Increased risk of leukemia
- GI conditions: tracheoesophageal fistula, imperforate anus
- Chronic otitis media
- Hearing and vision impairments

#### Manifestations

**Facial Features**
- Downward slanting eyes
- Low-set ears
- Flat nasal bridge
- Protruding tongue

**Body Features**
- Short, broad hands
- Simian line (transverse palmar and plantar crease)
- Broad, stubby feet with plantar crease
- Wide space between big and second toes
- Short, broad neck with excess skin
- Hyper-flexibility of joints and hypotonicity of muscles
- Dry skin

**Intellectual, Language, Social**
- Moderate to severe cognitive impairment
- Onset of Alzheimer's type dementia with increasing age
- Difficulty with grammar
- Social skills that surpass expected skills based on intellectual capacity
- Social skills and adaptive self-care skills that decline with age

#### Therapeutic Management
- No cure; treat symptoms and associated defects. May require surgery
- Regular evaluations of sight and hearing
- Periodic testing of thyroid, especially if growth is delayed
- More than 80% survive to age 30 and older

#### Nursing Care

**Emotional Support**
- Provide teaching regarding hypotonicity of muscles and hyperextensibility of joints
- Parents may interpret as signs of rejection
- Wrap in blanket before picking up

**Respiratory Management** (due to decreased muscle tone)
- Teach how to manage respiratory problems
- Prone to problems because of decreased muscle tone
- Inadequate drainage of mucus from nose
- Keep stuffy nose; breathe through mouth
- Use bulb syringe
- Increase fluid intake
- Use vaporizer
- Position changes

---

### TRISOMY 13 & TRISOMY 18

- More severe and debilitating than Down Syndrome
- Prognosis poor; many don't survive 1 year
- **Trisomy 13**: Microcephaly, severe intellectual disabilities, congenital heart defects, cleft lip/cleft palate
- **Trisomy 18**: Severe intellectual disabilities, congenital heart defects, low-set ears, overlapping fingers

---

### TURNER SYNDROME

#### Characteristics (Usually Female/XO)
- Webbed neck
- Low posterior hairline
- Wide-spaced nipples
- Edema of the hands and feet
- Amenorrhea (absence of menstruation)
- No development of secondary sex characteristics
- Sterility
- Perceptual and social skill difficulties

---

### KLINEFELTER SYNDROME

#### Characteristics (XXY Male)
- Decreased facial hair
- Gynecomastia (enlarged breast tissue)
- Decreased pubic hair
- Hypogonadism (low testosterone)
- Underdeveloped testes
- Taller than average by 5 years of age, with long legs and short torso
- Cognitive impairments of varying degree: motor delay, speech or language difficulties, attention deficits, and learning disabilities

---

### FRAGILE X SYNDROME

#### Manifestations
- Minor dysmorphic features
- Developmental delay/Intellectual impairment
- Behavior problems:
  - Attention deficits
  - Hand flapping and biting
  - Hyperactivity
  - Shyness and social isolation
  - Low self-esteem
  - Gaze aversion

---

### NEUROFIBROMATOSIS

#### Complications
- Headaches
- Hydrocephalus
- Scoliosis
- Cardiac defects, hypertension, seizures
- Vision and hearing loss
- Neurocognitive deficits
- Higher risk for neoplasms

---

### PHENYLKETONURIA (PKU)
### Phenylalanine Hydroxylase Deficiency

#### Definition
- Deficiency of phenylalanine hydroxylase, the hepatic enzyme needed to convert phenylalanine to tyrosine

#### Therapeutic Management
- Early detection through newborn screening
- Strict dietary management (low-phenylalanine diet)
- Regular monitoring of phenylalanine levels
- Lifelong dietary restrictions

#### Nursing Care
- Build a trusting relationship
- Stress the authenticity of the parents' feelings
- Reject personal biases
- Recognize that individuals cope in various ways; family behavior may not match expectations
- Help family identify their own strengths and supports
- Understand that family emotions may exhaust and disorganize them
- Assist family members to maintain open communication
- Provide referrals to local parent groups or other families with similar disorders
- Allow family to verbalize emotions and ask questions
- Always ask parents how they are doing

## PART 2: BEHAVIOR, COGNITION, OR DEVELOPMENT DISORDERS

### INTELLECTUAL AND DEVELOPMENTAL DISORDERS

#### General Characteristics
Children with these disorders may have limitations in both intellectual and adaptive functioning:
- Social interaction
- Use of language for self-expression
- Self-care abilities
- Lifelong challenges that require assistance from health care and educational professionals

#### Americans with Disabilities Act Definition
Developmental disability encompasses:
- Intellectual disability
- Sensory deficits
- Orthopedic problems
- Cerebral palsy
- Autism spectrum disorders

#### Causes of Intellectual Disability
- Genetic
- Alterations occurring during pregnancy
- Neonatal alterations
- Acquired childhood conditions or diseases
- Environmental problems
- Unknown causes

#### Management
- Increase the time spent in a regular school setting
- Multidisciplinary efforts
- Strong advocacy on the part of parents
- Address comorbidities
- Address safety challenges
- Address environmental challenges
- Ensure parental oversight

#### Terminology
- **Mental age**: Level of mental development
- **Functional age**: Ability to function in daily life
- **Adaptive functioning**: Ability to manage self-care and social skills
- **Intellectual impairment**: Reduced intellectual ability
- **Intellectual disability**: Diagnosed disorder affecting functioning
- **Autism spectrum disorders**: Range of developmental disorders

---

### ATTENTION DEFICIT HYPERACTIVITY DISORDER (ADHD)

#### Definition
- Inappropriate degrees of inattention, impulsiveness, and hyperactivity
- Problems associated with ADHD are usually school-related, involving academics
- Family and social relationships may be affected depending upon how they are able to cope with their problems

#### Diagnosis
- Need physical exam, vision and hearing screening, and detailed neurological assessment
- Need psychological testing
- Every child is different

#### Manifestations (DSM-V)

**Inattention**
- Carelessness, inattention to details
- Difficulty attending to work or games
- Does not listen well
- Poor follow-through with instructions
- Does not complete tasks
- Difficulty with organization skills
- Avoidance of tasks that require mental effort
- Misplaces equipment or supplies necessary to complete tasks
- Easily distracted
- Forgetfulness
- Poor planning

**Impulsivity/Hyperactivity**
- Fidgets with hands, feet, or hair
- Unable to remain in seat for extended periods
- Runs and climbs excessively in inappropriate settings
- Difficulty in engaging in quiet activities
- Mostly "on the go"
- Talks excessively
- Blurts out questions or answers
- Cannot await a turn
- Interrupts conversations

#### Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-V)
- Signs must be present for at least 6 months
- At least 2 symptoms have occurred before the age of 12 years
- Be present in two or more settings
- Not be associated with another mental or developmental disorder
- Significantly impair at least one level of functioning (academic, social, occupational)
- Not all exhibit overt hyperactivity, but most demonstrate a degree of impulsivity
- Frequently co-morbid with other disorders

#### Therapeutic Management

**Goals**
- Reduce frequency and intensity of unsocialized behaviors
- Treatment interventions targeted at enhancing child's capabilities and self-esteem

**Pharmacotherapy**
- Methylphenidate (Ritalin)
- Dextroamphetamine (Dexedrine)
- Amphetamine/dextroamphetamine (Adderall)
- Newer medications with once-daily dosing:
  - Concerta, Ritalin LA, Metadate ER (methylphenidate formulations)
  - Adderall XR
- Initial dose should be lowest appropriate for child's age and weight, then increased over weeks until symptoms controlled
- Often combined with behavior-oriented family therapy

**Behavioral Therapy**
1. Families are instructed on various techniques:
   - Positive reinforcement
   - Rewarding small increments of desired behaviors
   - Age-appropriate consequences of undesired behaviors
   - Organization charts
2. Counselors can help children work through problematic situations

#### Nursing Care

**Assessment**
- Document child's typical behavior
- Child's development and family history explored in detail
- Observation within the home or school setting
- Understand parenting challenges: fewer positive parenting experiences, decreased parenting self-confidence, increased stress

**Intervention**
- Teach family about disorder and help develop management strategies
- Provide instructions about medications
- Monitor appetite: if concerned, give medication after meals instead of before and provide nutritious "on the go" snacks
- Monitor for side effects:
  - Tricyclic antidepressants increase incidence of dental caries
  - Anticholinergic action increases saliva viscosity and produces dry mouth
  - Address with: fluids, limited refined carbs, dentist visits, artificial saliva

### AUTISM

#### Definition
- Complex neurobiological developmental disorder accompanied by a broad range of intellectual, social, and behavioral problems
- Developmental delays in language and communication skills
- Abnormalities in language when it does develop
- Significant impairments in reciprocal social behavior
- Restricted and narrow repertoire of interests and behaviors
- Ritualized and repetitive behavior patterns
- Stereotyped, destructive, and disruptive behavior

#### Manifestations

**Early "Red Flags"**
- No big smiles or joyful expression by 6 months
- No babbling by 12 months
- No words by 16 months
- Any loss of babbling or speech at any age

**Impairment in Developmental Areas**
- Communication or language
- Social skills
- Behavior
- May have cognitive capacity, but also have:
  - Absence or delay in language
  - Abnormal ways of relating to people, objects, or events
  - Difficulty regulating emotions

**Impairment in Social Interactions**
- Marked impairment in eye-to-eye contact, facial expression, and gestures
- Failure to develop peer relationships appropriate for age
- A lack of spontaneous seeking to share enjoyment or interests with other people
- A lack of social or emotional reciprocity

**Impairment in Language/Communication**
- Delay in or total lack of the development of spoken language
- Marked impairment in the ability to initiate or sustain a conversation with others
- Repetitive use of language: echolalia or parrot speech
- Lack of make-believe play or social imitative play

**Restricted Repetitive and Stereotyped Patterns of Behavior, Interests, and Activities**
- Persistent preoccupation with parts of an object
- Stereotyped and repetitive motor mannerisms
- Odd repetitive behaviors
- Hysterical responses to minor changes in the environment
- Behaviors that are self-injurious

#### Diagnosis
- Based on specific behavioral manifestations
- Developmental screening at well visits: 9, 18, 24, and 30 months
- Autism-specific screening at 18 and 24 months
- Assessment tools:
  - Ages and Stages Questionnaires (ASQ)
  - Communication and Symbolic Behavior Scales – Developmental Profile (CSBS-DP)
  - Pervasive Developmental Disorders Screening Test II (PDDST-II)
  - Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers (M-CHAT)
- Parental concerns and observations
- Intelligence testing

#### Therapeutic Management
- Create environment that facilitates interaction and promotes replacement of stereotypical behaviors with more normal behaviors
- Specialized programs
- Facilitative communication: picture boards or keyboards

#### Nursing Care

**Assessment**
- Physically assess as normal
- Systematic exploration of child's skills: feeding, dressing, toileting
- Interactive patterns and verbalization skills
- Motor skills
- Work closely with family to determine child's routines, habits, and preferences, communication techniques
- Specific cues
- Monitor signs of anxiety or emotional comfort
- Evaluate safety: helmets or side rolls

**Intervention**
- Work with the child one on one
- Protect from injury
- Assign limited number of caregivers
- Give positive reinforcement when appropriate
- Anticipate needs until communication can be established
- In hospital setting:
  - Decrease stimuli by providing private room
  - Avoid extraneous auditory and visual distractions
  - Encourage parents to bring child's possessions
  - Minimal holding and eye contact may be necessary
  - Introduce slowly to new situations
  - Short visits by staff caregivers whenever possible
  - Need to be told directly what to do
  - Communication should be at child's developmental level, brief, and concrete

### FETAL ALCOHOL SPECTRUM DISORDER (FASD)

#### Definition
- Prenatal alcohol exposure resulting in range from mild to severe disorders
- FASD is the most severe disorder

#### Classic Defects
- Persistent symmetric growth retardation
- Malformations of face and skull
- Skeletal and cardiac malformation
- CNS deficits: intellectual and developmental disabilities

#### Diagnostic Criteria

**Three Facial Abnormalities**
- Smooth philtrum
- Thin vermilion border
- Small palpebral fissures

**Growth Deficit**
- 10th percentile or less for height, weight, or both

**CNS Abnormalities**
- Head circumference at 10th percentile or less
- Brain abnormalities identified by imaging studies
- Motor deficits or seizures with no other identified cause
- Developmental milestones below expected range for child's age

**Physical or Psychosocial Circumstances**
- In at least three of the following categories

#### Physical Manifestations

**Physical Defects**
- Growth retardation
- Facial abnormalities
- Skeletal deformities
- Cardiac defects

**Brain and CNS Problems**
- Intellectual disability
- Developmental delays
- Seizures
- Motor deficits
- Neurobehavioral abnormalities

**Social and Behavioral Issues**
- Difficulty with social interaction
- Behavioral problems
- Learning disabilities
- Attention deficits

#### Nursing Care

**Assessment and Monitoring**
- Extensive diagnostic workup
- Monitor weight gain, intake and output
- Various feeding strategies

**Intervention**
- Assist parents with feedings
- Discuss neurological and developmental deficits with parents
- Avoid encouraging unrealistic expectations: acknowledge existing problems and suggest coping strategies
- Support mother: she may feel guilt or blame by other family members
- Encourage verbalization of feelings
- Initiate referrals to appropriate community resources
- Provide long-term follow-up

#### Prenatal Teaching

Important information for pregnant women:
- Alcohol enters your bloodstream and reaches your developing fetus by crossing the placenta
- Alcohol causes higher blood alcohol concentrations in your developing baby than in your body because a fetus metabolizes alcohol slower than an adult does
- Alcohol interferes with the delivery of oxygen and optimal nutrition to your developing baby
- Exposure to alcohol before birth can harm the development of tissues and organs and cause permanent brain damage in your baby

## PART 3: CHILD ABUSE

### Definition
- Includes: Emotional, Physical, and Sexual exploitation or molestation
- Neglect: Deliberate failure to provide for a child's physical, educational, or emotional needs
- No single etiologic factor is responsible for abuse; it is an interaction between several factors

### Risk Factors for Child Maltreatment
- Perpetrator alcohol or drug abuse
- Domestic abuse
- Child disability
- Socioeconomic factors: economic hardship, lack of resources

### Manifestations

#### Physical Abuse Indicators
- Bruises in various stages of healing; bite marks
- Burns in unusual locations
- Skeletal injury
- Child's behavior: wariness or fear of adults, may resist going home
- Inconsistent stories between child and parent
- Parents demonstrate inconsistent or overly harsh discipline

#### Neglect Indicators
- Inadequate weight gain for age
- Poor growth pattern
- Failure to thrive
- Comes to school inappropriately dressed
- Signs of inadequate hygiene
- Lacking routine healthcare
- Truancy
- May beg for money or steal food
- Report being home alone for long periods
- Parent-child interaction reveals parent who appears indifferent or unaware of child's needs or demonstrates behaviors of possible emotional or substance abuse disorder

#### Emotional Abuse Indicators
- Delays in physical and emotional development
- Demonstrate ways that are "too adult" in relation to child's age, or too immature for age
- Behavioral extremes: overly aggressive or overly compliant
- Repetitive habits: head banging, rocking, biting, sucking
- Parent-child relationship appears to lack warmth or attachment
- Parents demonstrate overly critical approach

#### Sexual Abuse Indicators
- Difficulty walking or sitting
- Complain of pain on urination or in genital area
- Physical signs of bruising or laceration of perineal tissue
- Diagnosis of sexually transmitted disease
- Nightmares
- Decreased appetite
- Sudden refusal to participate in physical activities
- Overt aggression
- Emotional distress or self-destructive behaviors
- May use sexual language and innuendo not appropriate for age

#### Special Forms of Abuse

**Abusive Head Trauma (Shaken Baby Syndrome)**
- Violent shaking of infants/young children
- Can cause serious brain injury or death

**Factitious Disorder by Proxy (Munchausen Syndrome by Proxy)**
- Parent or caregiver falsifies illness in child
- Diagnosis occurs when symptoms disappear when the abuser is not present

### Characteristics of Abusive Family

**Family Environment**
- Isolation from community and social groups
- Intense competition for emotional resources within family
- Low trust of outsiders and family members
- Unpredictable and unstable family environment
- Conflict resolution generally achieved through aggression or power struggle between family members
- Communication often characterized by mixed or double messages, threats, or focus on nonverbal communication rather than direct verbalization
- Family roles that are typically fixed and traditional with rigid rules
- Frequent domination by a single family member who maintains control through manipulation, intimidation, deceit, and aggression

### Characteristics of the Child

The child may unintentionally contribute to the abusive situation because of:
- Temperament
- Position in the family
- Additional physical needs due to illness and/or disability
- Activity level
- Degree of sensitivity to parental needs
- Child may remind them of someone they dislike

### Nursing Care

#### Identify Abusive Situations Early
- Observe caregiver-child interaction
- Conduct detailed history and interview

#### Protect the Child from Further Abuse
- Remove from unsafe environment
- Report to appropriate authorities
- Document findings carefully

#### Support the Child
- Provide emotional support
- Reassure safety
- Listen without judgment

#### Support the Family
- Approach non-judgmentally
- Provide resources
- Connect with support services

#### Plan for Discharge
- Coordinate with social services
- Develop safety plan
- Identify community resources

#### Prevent Abuse
- Educate parents about child development
- Teach appropriate discipline
- Provide parenting resources

#### Talking to Children Who Reveal Abuse

**Critical Approach**
- Provide privacy
- Do not promise to keep it secret
- Use their vocabulary to discuss body parts
- Reassure them that they have done nothing wrong
- Tell them the abuse was not their fault

## PART 4: INFECTIOUS DISEASES

### General Terminology

- **Incubation Period**: Interval between infection or exposure and development of symptoms
- **Prodromal**: Interval between early manifestations and when actual disease is evident
- **Communicability**: Time during which an infectious agent may be transferred directly or indirectly from an infected person

---

### CHICKEN POX / VARICELLA

#### Description
- Starts as a rash that can spread to the rest of the body
- Begins as macules and progresses to vesicles that crust and fall off
- Virus remains in the body for life and may reappear as shingles, especially in the elderly

#### Etiology
- Varicella zoster virus

#### Communicability and Transmission
- **Communicability**: One day prior to eruption of vesicles to 6 days after the first vesicles have crusted
- **Transmission**: Direct contact with the fluid in the vesicles or droplets from an infected person's nose or throat (droplet nuclei)

#### Manifestations
- Low-grade fever, malaise, anorexia
- Extremely pruritic (itchy) skin eruptions:
  - **Macules**: Discolored spots on the skin
  - **Papules**: Red elevated lesion
  - **Vesicles**: Blister lesion with inflamed base containing serous fluid
  - **Crusts**: Dried lesions
- Eruptions may continue to develop for 2-3 days
- Begin on trunk, spreading to face and extremities
- Lesions may breakout on mucous membranes
- Temperature rises
- Lymphadenopathy may develop

#### Management and Prevention

**Precautions**
- Standard, airborne, and contact precautions if hospitalized

**Pharmacological**
- Acetaminophen for fever (avoid aspirin—Reye syndrome risk; avoid ibuprofen—necrotizing fasciitis risk)
- Acyclovir for severe cases (best if begun within 24 hours of rash)

**Comfort Measures**
- Control itching with:
  - Oral antihistamines
  - Lotions: calamine, Cetaphil, or any lotion with pramoxine
  - Warm bath with baking soda or oatmeal preparations
- Keep cool
- Keep fingernails short and clean
- May need socks on hands

**Prevention**
- Varicella Vaccine

#### Complications
- Secondary bacterial infections:
  - Abscesses
  - Cellulitis
  - Necrotizing fasciitis
  - Pneumonia
  - Sepsis

---

### RUBEOLA / MEASLES

#### Etiology
- Highly contagious virus

#### Communicability and Transmission
- **Communicability**: 4 days before the rash to 5 days after the rash
- **Transmission**: Contact with respiratory tract secretions, blood, and urine of infected person

#### Manifestations

**Prodromal Stage** (24 hours prior)
- Fever, malaise, anorexia
- Coryza (inflammation of nasal mucous membrane with discharge)
- Cough
- Conjunctivitis ("pink eye")
- Koplik spots (white spots on oral mucosa—pathognomonic sign)

**Rash Stage**
- Erythematous maculopapular rash on face that spreads downward
- After 3-4 days rash becomes brown and desquamation (scaling) occurs
- Photophobia (light sensitivity)

#### Prevention and Treatment

**Prevention**
- MMR vaccine

**Management**
- Isolation until 5th day of rash (airborne precautions)
- Cool mist vaporizer
- Dim lights
- Clean eyelids with saline
- Vitamin A supplementation
- Prevent rubbing eyes
- Acetaminophen for fever
- Liquids and soft, bland foods
- Antibiotics for high-risk children
- Do not give aspirin or ibuprofen

#### Complications
- Otitis media
- Pneumonia
- Obstructive laryngitis
- Laryngotracheitis

---

### MUMPS

#### Etiology
- Virus

#### Communicability and Transmission
- **Communicability**: Immediately before and after swelling begins
- **Transmission**: Direct contact with saliva or droplet

#### Manifestations
- Fever, headache, malaise for 24 hours
- Ear ache aggravated by chewing
- Pain, tenderness, and enlargement of parotid glands (cheeks)

#### Prevention and Treatment

**Prevention**
- MMR vaccine

**Management**
- Droplet and contact precautions if hospitalized
- Analgesics for pain
- Antipyretics for fever
- Intravenous fluids if refuses to eat
- Soft, bland foods
- Hot/cold compresses to neck
- Relief for Orchitis: warmth and support with tight-fitting underwear

#### Complications
- Deafness
- Encephalitis
- Meningitis
- Epididymo-orchitis (males)

---

### SCARLET FEVER

#### Etiology
- Group A beta-hemolytic streptococcus

#### Transmission
- Direct contact with infected person or droplet spread
- Indirectly through contact with contaminated articles or ingestion of contaminated milk or other food

#### Manifestations
- Abrupt high fever
- Tachycardia
- Headache
- Swollen tonsils covered with exudate
- White strawberry tongue (Day 1 and 2), becomes red strawberry tongue (Day 4 or 5)
- Generalized rash: red pinhead-sized punctate lesions
- Circumoral pallor (pale area around mouth)
- Desquamation (scaling) after one week

#### Treatment and Management

**Antibiotics**
- Take full course of antibiotics—compliance is essential

**Precautions**
- Standard and droplet precautions until 24 hours after initiation of treatment

**Comfort Measures**
- Comfort measures for sore throat
- Soft, bland diet

#### Complications
- Acute glomerulonephritis
- Rheumatic fever
- Sinusitis

---

### ERYTHEMA INFECTIOSUM / FIFTH DISEASE

#### Manifestations
- Coryza (runny nose)
- Fever, headache
- Rash: "slapped cheek" appearance
- Three-stage rash: slapped cheek, maculopapular that subsides

#### Etiology
- Virus

#### Complications
- Fetal death if occurs during pregnancy

---

### RUBELLA / GERMAN MEASLES

#### Etiology
- Virus

#### Communicability and Transmission
- **Communicability**: 7 days pre-rash to 5 days post-rash
- **Transmission**: Nasopharyngeal secretions

#### Manifestations

**Clinical Manifestations**
- Low-grade fever, headache, malaise, and anorexia
- Mild conjunctivitis
- Coryza (runny nose), sore throat, cough
- Maculopapular rash on face spreading downward
- Rash usually subsides one day after appearing
- Prodromal stage present in adolescents/adults

#### Management

**Prevention**
- MMR vaccine

**Management**
- Antipyretics for fever
- Analgesics for pain
- Isolation from pregnant women (teratogenic)

#### Complications
- Rare but teratogenic effects on fetus

---

### PERTUSSIS / WHOOPING COUGH

#### Etiology
- Bacteria

#### Communicability and Transmission
- **Communicability**: Before onset of coughing
- **Transmission**: Direct contact or droplet spread from infected person; indirect contact with freshly contaminated articles

#### Manifestations

**Initial Presentation**
- Appears to be upper respiratory infection (URI)
- Dry hacking cough increasing in severity

**Paroxysmal Stage**
- Period of short rapid coughing followed by sudden inspirations associated with a high-pitched crowing sound (paroxysms)
- Flushing of cheeks, can be cyanotic due to losing breath while coughing
- Bulging eyes
- Protruding tongue
- Possible vomiting

#### Management

**Hospitalization**
- Oxygen
- Humidifier
- Intravenous fluids
- Droplet precautions

**Care Measures**
- Keep cool to prevent coughing
- Observe for airway obstruction
- Monitor breath sounds

#### Complications
- Pneumonia
- Otitis media
- Atelectasis

---

### ROSEOLA (EXANTHEM SUBITUM)

#### Communicability
- Unknown

#### Manifestations
- High temperature for 3-4 days in child who appears well
- Temperature decreases and nonpruritic (non-itchy) rash appears on trunk, then spreads to face and extremities
- Rash lasts 1-2 days

#### Complications
- Febrile seizures

---

### IMMUNIZATIONS

#### General Information
- May cause mild fever or soreness and redness at the injection site
- Some cause rash or stiff joints (MMR)
- Hepatitis B: if mother is positive, baby gets Immunoglobulin
- TdaP/DtaP: Side effects include irritability, loss of appetite (may subside in day or two); more serious complications include seizures
- Should not be given when child is ill
- Acellular vaccines: have fragments of cells to stimulate an immune response
- Toxoids: use a toxin made by the germ that causes a disease; immune response is targeted to the toxin

#### Documentation Requirements
- Name of vaccine
- Lot number
- Site of injection
- Date of administration
- Consent obtained
- Vaccine information sheets: provide the purpose of the vaccine, possible side effects, and answer questions caregivers may have

#### Special Considerations
- Meningococcal: generally given to those going to college
- Know what immunizations are given when
- Compliance with immunization schedule is important
